# Black Rot (Organic Management Guide)

Organic control of black rot is notoriously difficult because the fungus is highly aggressive, but it can be managed successfully using specific OMRI-listed treatments.

Organic sprays are preventative, not curative, so they must be applied thoroughly before rain events and before symptoms appear.

## Top Organic Sprays for Black Rot

### 1) Copper Fungicides (Organic Standard)

**How it works**: Copper inhibits fungal spore germination on plant surfaces.

**Products to look for**:
- Southern Ag Liquid Copper Fungicide (walmart.com)
- Monterey Liqui-Cop (domyown.com)

**Caveat**:
- Continuous use can build up copper in soil.
- Some grape varieties (especially native American varieties like Concord) can be sensitive to copper injury.

### 2) Biofungicides (Bacillus Strains)

**How it works**: Beneficial bacteria colonize plant tissue, outcompete black rot spores, and can trigger natural plant defenses.

**Products to look for**:
- Arber Organic Bio Fungicide (arber.com), using Bacillus amyloliquefaciens
- Serenade, using Bacillus subtilis

**Advantage**:
- Safe for pollinators, pets, and people
- Zero harvest restrictions

### 3) Potassium Bicarbonate

**How it works**: Changes leaf and berry surface pH, creating an environment unfavorable to fungal growth.

**Products to look for**:
- MilStop
- Armicarb O

**Note**:
- Moderately effective on its own
- Works best when rotated with copper sprays

## Strict Application Timeline for Next Spring

Organic options often fail if applied only after rot appears. Protect new growth early using this schedule:

- Bud burst: clean vine
- Shoots 3-5 inches long: first spray
- Pre-bloom: second spray (critical)
- Post-bloom/fruit set: third spray (most critical for baby berries)
- Through mid-summer: continue every 7-10 days

### Spray Timing Details

- **First spray**: As soon as green shoots are 3 to 5 inches long in early spring
- **Second spray**: Just before flower buds open
- **Third spray**: Immediately after petals fall (fruit set)
- **Cover treatments**: Every 7 to 10 days through mid-summer

If heavy rain occurs, reapply as soon as weather clears because rain removes organic barrier protection.

## Special Case: Black Rot Spores Under a Deck

If rotted grape fruit has fallen under an inaccessible deck, avoid dismantling the structure. Shift your strategy toward physical barriers and canopy-focused prevention.

### 1) Strip the "Launch Zone" Clean (Trunk Stripping)

**Action**:
- Remove every green shoot, leaf, and sucker from the main trunk for the lower 3 to 4 feet above the deck floor.

**Why it works**:
- Spores usually move only about 2 to 3 feet upward from ground sources.
- A bare-trunk buffer encourages spores to land on bark instead of vulnerable green tissue.

### 2). Apply a Liquid Shield: Lime-Sulfur Paste (Whitewash)

**The Problem**:
- If old grapevines are tightly twisted and woven into a fence, fitting a rigid plastic tree guard around the trunks is nearly impossible.

**The Action**:
- Mix a thick, paste-like whitewash using hydrated lime, water, and an organic lime-sulfur fungicide. Use a cheap paint brush to coat the bottom 3 feet of the twisted trunks and the surrounding fence contact points until they are completely covered.

**Why it works**:
- This traditional orchard technique creates a heavy, alkaline crust directly over the bark. It chemically neutralizes any black rot spores the moment they splash onto the trunk from under the deck, completely eliminating the need for a physical plastic guard while allowing the vine to stay safely woven into the fence.

### 3) Focus Sprays Upward

**Action**:
- Since canopy growth is 6 feet or higher, focus preventative sprays on upper foliage.

**Why it works**:
- Thorough upper-canopy coverage (especially leaf undersides) forms your main protective shield when source cleanup is not possible under the deck.
