## 1. Vladimir Putin and Russian Orthodoxy

Vladimir Putin identifies publicly as an Orthodox Christian, though historians, political scientists, and religious scholars widely view his religiosity as a mix of personal practice and political strategy.

* **Public Persona & Claims:**
  * **Baptism:** Putin has stated that he was secretly baptized as an infant by his mother in Leningrad (Saint Petersburg) during the Soviet era.
  * **Public Practice:** He frequently attends Russian Orthodox services (notably Easter and Christmas), visits monasteries, and wears a baptismal cross.
  * **Church Alliance:** Since returning to the presidency in 2012, he has aligned himself closely with the Russian Orthodox Church and its leader, Patriarch Kirill.

* **Political & Ideological Usage:**
  * **State Identity:** Putin uses Orthodox Christianity to promote traditional cultural values, frame Russia as a defender of conservative morality against the West, and foster domestic national unity.
  * ***Russkiy Mir* ("Russian World"):** His political ideology links Orthodox faith with a shared historical identity encompassing Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus (*Holy Rus'*), which he uses to justify geopolitical ambitions and military campaigns.

* **Analysis by Experts:**
  * Scholars generally describe Putin’s engagement with Orthodoxy as **instrumental and utilitarian**—using church hierarchy and doctrine as an extension of soft power and political control rather than adhering strictly to traditional theological practice.

## 2. Volodymyr Zelenskyy's Religious Background

Unlike Vladimir Putin, Volodymyr Zelenskyy is **Jewish**, though he is largely secular in his personal practice.

* **Jewish Background:**
  * **Heritage:** Zelenskyy was born to Jewish parents in Kryvyi Rih and lost family members in the Holocaust. He often describes his background as a typical "ordinary Soviet Jewish upbringing," where state anti-religious policies meant the family was not religiously observant.
  * **Personal Beliefs:** He considers faith a private matter. He has stated that while he believes in God, he does not publicly discuss religion or adhere strictly to formal religious practices.

* **Contrast in Approach to Religion:**
  * **Political Separation:** Unlike Putin—who uses Orthodox Christianity as a cornerstone of state identity and geopolitical ideology—Zelenskyy treats religion as a strictly personal choice. He rarely invoked his faith during his presidential campaign and avoids using religion as a tool for national mobilization.
  * **Family Life:** His family reflects Ukraine’s mixed cultural environment; his wife, Olena Zelenska, is non-Jewish, and their children were baptized.

## 3. The Zelenskyy Family & Baptism Details

The reference to Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Olena Zelenska's children being baptized comes from details revealed by Olena Zelenska in Ukrainian media interviews regarding their family's religious practices:

* **Background:** Olena Zelenska (née Kiyashko) comes from an Orthodox Christian background, while Volodymyr Zelenskyy was raised in a secular Jewish household.
* **The Baptism:** When the couple decided to have their children—their daughter Oleksandra (born 2004) and son Kyrylo (born 2013)—baptized in the Eastern Orthodox tradition, Olena realized that she herself had not been baptized as a child.
* **Olena's Own Baptism:** To proceed with baptizing the children, church requirements led Olena to undergo baptism as an adult at the same time. Ukrainian actress and close family friend Olena Kravets served as her godmother during the ceremony.
* **Zelenskyy's Role:** Volodymyr Zelenskyy did not convert to Christianity; he supported his wife's cultural and religious choices for their children while remaining secular himself.

## 4. Initial Reflection: Isaiah 60:3 and the Russia-Ukraine Conflict

Today’s verse—**Isaiah 60:3** (*“Nations will come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your dawn”*)—offers several poignant points of reflection when applied to the ongoing Russia-Ukraine war:

* **The Contrast of Light in "Deep Darkness":** Isaiah 60 opens against a backdrop of deep darkness and gloom covering the earth. War, territorial destruction, human suffering, and propaganda represent that exact kind of darkness. The verse reminds us that true "light" is characterized by truth, justice, and moral clarity—qualities that stand in sharp contrast to the chaotic darkness of armed conflict.
* **A Vision of True Leadership ("Kings"):** The prophecy speaks of "kings" (world leaders and decision-makers) being drawn toward light and righteousness rather than seeking power through force or imperial expansion.
  * **Coercion vs. Attraction:** War attempts to subjugate nations through force. Isaiah’s vision describes leaders voluntarily moving toward a moral beacon of peace and justice.
  * **Accountability to Divine Truth:** It serves as a reminder that political leaders are ultimately accountable to a standard higher than military might.
* **International Unity Built on Peace, Not Power:** "Nations shall come..." points to a global order where countries unite around shared light—restoration, human dignity, and justice. Rather than alliances forged purely out of geopolitical fear or military dominance, true security and international solidarity arise when nations rally around principles of peace and national sovereignty.
* **The Hope for Long-Term Restoration:** In the broader context of Isaiah 60, the passage leads into a promise that *"violence shall no longer be heard in your land, neither wasting nor destruction within your borders"* (v. 18). Verse 3 represents the "dawn" of that hope. For communities living under the shadow of destruction in Ukraine, it offers a spiritual reminder that darkness is not the final state—reconstruction, peace, and ultimate justice remain the enduring promise.

## 5. Overview of the Crusades

The Crusades were a series of religious and political wars fought primarily between Western European Christians and Muslim powers from 1095 to 1291 over control of the Holy Land (Jerusalem and the Levant).

* **Key Drivers & Motivations:**
  * **Religious Zeal:** Pope Urban II launched the First Crusade in 1095, promising full forgiveness of sins (*plenary indulgence*) to Christians who fought to reclaim Jerusalem and assist the Byzantine Empire against Seljuk Turk expansion.
  * **Geopolitical & Economic Expansion:** European nobles sought new territory, glory, and trade routes, while the Papacy saw an opportunity to consolidate religious authority across Europe.
  * **The Concept of Holy War:** The campaigns merged pilgrimage with warfare, framing violence as a penitential act sanctioned by God.

* **Major Campaigns:**
  * **First Crusade (1096–1099):** The most successful for Western forces. Crusaders captured Jerusalem in 1099 and established four Crusader States (including the Kingdom of Jerusalem).
  * **Second Crusade (1147–1149):** Launched after the fall of Edessa; ended in failure for the European forces after an unsuccessful siege of Damascus.
  * **Third Crusade (1189–1192):** Prompted by Saladin’s recapture of Jerusalem in 1187. Led by Richard the Lionheart and Saladin, it ended in a treaty permitting unarmed Christian pilgrims to visit Jerusalem, though Muslims retained control.
  * **Fourth Crusade (1202–1204):** Diverted from its original goal, the Crusader army infamously sacked the Christian city of Constantinople, severely weakening the Byzantine Empire.
  * **Later Crusades:** Subscribed to varying targets (Egypt, Tunis), gradually losing momentum until the fall of the final Crusader stronghold, Acre, in 1291.

* **Long-Term Impact:**
  * **Socio-Religious Divides:** Deepened lasting hostility and mistrust between Western Christianity, Eastern Orthodoxy, and Islam.
  * **Cultural & Economic Exchange:** Introduced Western Europe to Middle Eastern trade goods, medicine, philosophy, science, and classical texts preserved in Islamic libraries, helping spark the Renaissance.
  * **Consolidation of Royal Power:** Monarchies gained strength as knightly families lost wealth and lands to finance the campaigns.

## 6. Understanding Jihad

**Jihad** (literal Arabic: "striving" or "struggling") is a foundational concept in Islam with two main dimensions that contrast sharply with the medieval Western concept of Crusades.

* **Internal vs. External Jihad:**
  * **Greater Jihad (*Jihad al-Nafs*):** The primary, spiritual struggle within an individual to resist temptation, purify one's soul, live uprightly, and uphold Islamic principles in daily life.
  * **Lesser Jihad (*Jihad al-Asghar*):** The physical or military effort to defend the Muslim community (*Ummah*) against aggression, oppression, or religious persecution.

* **Rules and Justifications of Military Jihad:**
  * **Defensive Premise:** Classical Islamic legal jurisprudence (*Fiqh*) strictly limits military action to defensive circumstances or protecting freedom of worship, forbidding unprovoked aggression.
  * **Rules of Engagement:** Historical Islamic law establishes strict rules for military conflict, forbidding the targeting of non-combatants (women, children, the elderly, clergy), the destruction of crops and livestock, and the desecration of places of worship.
  * **State Authority:** Historically, declaring a legitimate military jihad required authorization from a recognized sovereign ruler or judicial authority, rather than individual groups.

* **Comparison: Crusades vs. Military Jihad:**

| Feature | The Crusades | Military Jihad |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Goal** | Reclaiming Holy Sites / Territorial expansion for Christendom | Defense of faith, community, and territorial sovereignty |
| **Religious Merit** | Plenary indulgence (direct forgiveness of all temporal sins) | Spiritual duty to protect justice; martyrdom for defenders |
| **Modern Usage** | Rarely used in modern military contexts | Distorted by extremist non-state actors to justify political violence |

## 7. Comprehensive Synthesis: Isaiah 60:3 Across Historical & Contemporary Conflicts

Isaiah 60:3 (*“Nations will come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your dawn”*) provides a critical perspective for examining both medieval holy wars and modern geopolitical conflicts.

* **The Distorting of "Light" for Imperial Power:**
  * **Historical Crusades & Medieval Jihad:** Popes and medieval rulers framed military conquest as a sacred duty, promising spiritual rewards for fighting over physical territory.
  * **Modern Russia-Ukraine Conflict:** The concept of *Russkiy Mir* ("Russian World") weaves Orthodox Christianity with state sovereignty to cast military aggression as a civilizational mission.
  * *Insight:* Isaiah’s vision suggests that true light does not conquer through force or coercion; rather, it attracts leaders and societies by offering genuine justice, peace, and human dignity.

* **State Identity vs. Universal Truth:**
  * **Instrumentalizing Religion:** When state power absorbs religious doctrine, faith becomes a weapon to divide ("us vs. them") rather than a universal light that holds leaders accountable to a higher standard of justice.
  * **The Call for Leadership:** Isaiah 60 implies that true authority ("kings") lies in orienting a nation toward ethical light rather than using religious heritage to enforce political compliance.

* **From Holy Territory to Universal Peace:**
  * Across centuries of religious and national conflict, fighting has centered on controlling physical borders, sacred sites, or geopolitical spheres of influence. Isaiah’s prophecy redefines ultimate security: it moves the focus away from territorial dominance and points toward a future where international order is grounded in shared moral truth, mutual respect, and lasting restoration.
